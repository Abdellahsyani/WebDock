Chapter 4

The Command Chain:
Daemons & Runtimes

When you type a command, who actually builds the Namespaces and cgroups? Let's meet the hierarchy of background processes that run the show.

Docker CLI
⟶
dockerd
⟶
containerd
⟶
runc

1. dockerd (The Boss)

The Docker Daemon (dockerd) runs 24/7 in the background. It listens to the Unix socket (/var/run/docker.sock) for commands from your terminal.

It handles all the high-level tasks: downloading images, setting up virtual networks, and managing volumes. However, it no longer builds the containers itself. It delegates that job down the chain.

2. containerd (The Manager)

When dockerd receives a request, it hands the image blueprint to containerd. This daemon is specifically designed to manage the lifecycle of containers.

It prepares the environment and tells the lowest-level tools to execute. Because containerd is an independent industry standard, massive orchestrators like Kubernetes use it directly, bypassing dockerd entirely!

3. runc & shim (The Builders)

runc is the software that actually talks to the Kernel to create Namespaces, cgroups, and execute chroot. But here is the catch: it is not a daemon! It builds the container and immediately exits.

The containerd-shim

If runc exits, who watches the running container? A tiny background process called a shim is left behind to babysit the container and keep its logs open. Because of shims, you can completely restart the Docker Daemon, and your containers will stay alive!
